# Final Architecture Decision Checkpoint

This checkpoint resolves the decision backlog from audits B–J.

## 1. Buildings and adult facilities
Only mechanically meaningful adult facilities become standalone buildings. Other functions should be modules, rooms, upgrades, event unlocks, or visual variations of larger facilities.

## 2. Equipment philosophy
Use few impactful equipment slots, with limited specialized slots unlockable through progression where useful. Equipment should change playstyle, skill interactions, defenses, roles, or capabilities rather than form a large linear stat ladder.

## 3. Transmutation
Adopt reversible transmutation with a meaningful but non-punitive cost, plus the ability to maintain multiple saved configurations where implementation supports it. Transmutation exists for specialization and trade-offs, not irreversible trap choices.

## 4. Aptitude presentation
Show aptitude as a concise combination of qualitative descriptor and rank, e.g. Competent — Rank II.

## 5. Aptitude retention
Experience and learned aptitude are never lost. Characters may switch to mechanically inferior roles without forgetting prior expertise. Natural aptitude affects learning speed and/or performance but does not invalidate later retraining.

## 6. Exploration presentation
Use a hybrid model: strategic region/destination selection plus explorable maps and encounters. Procedural generation should support repeatable exploration, gathering maps, and potentially selected quests. Hand-authored maps are reserved for narratively important levels, major story moments, and content where authored structure adds clear value.

## 7. Retreat and failure
Retreat is possible, but failure can cost contextual opportunities or rewards. The default is not permanent punishment or universal game over.

## 8. Progression and gating
Use a combination centered on two rules:
- advanced upgrades and possibility layers are unlocked by main-quest progress;
- some resources exist only in regions unlocked during later acts.

Players may see a missing requirement or resource and correctly infer that narrative progress or newly opened regions will reveal it. Stockpiling and optimization remain possible within the currently unlocked layer, but the next layer cannot be fully accessed before its narrative gate.

## 9. Gate visibility and quest rewards
Systemic requirements may be visible, while narrative discoveries remain partially mysterious. Quests may preview or hint at major rewards so players can intentionally pursue desired progression paths without eliminating discovery.

## 10. Currency
Adopt a single common currency: Gold. Gold is used for conventional purchases such as items, seeds, and other trade goods. It complements material resources rather than replacing them.

## 11. Obsolete equipment
Prefer evolution/transmutation and continued build relevance over mandatory disposal. Older equipment may remain useful for alternate builds and can be upgraded or transformed when appropriate.

## 12. Enemy scaling
Use mostly fixed/content-authored power bands plus behavioral, encounter, or variant changes. Avoid universal automatic scaling. Difficulty should increasingly come from identity, tactics, interactions, and encounter composition rather than raw numbers.

## 13. Respec and build experimentation
Respec is available through specific facilities or moments and carries a light cost. Experimentation should have some friction but not trap the player permanently.

## 14. Harmony feedback
Use a qualitative global direction indicator plus visible regional consequences. Avoid a deterministic universal percentage. The player can understand the broad direction of their journey while seeing concrete local results.
