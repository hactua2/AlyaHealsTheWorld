# Translated World and Narrative Premise

## Source-derived premise

The source establishes a fantasy world containing magical peoples and creatures and a substantial unexplored frontier referred to as the wilds. The central gameplay premise is that conflicts, including combat, can have an alternative non-lethal or relationship-driven route of resolution rather than relying exclusively on conventional defeat.

## Neutral implementation premise

The implementation-facing game is a fantasy RPG in which the protagonist travels through inhabited regions and unexplored wilds, resolves local conflicts, builds relationships and alliances, and expands a growing community.

Conflicts support at least two systemic resolution paths:

- conventional Health defeat;
- Moral depletion leading to Surrender and a post-combat narrative resolution.

This document preserves the structural premise, not the source-specific presentation of relationship or intimacy themes.

## World content supported by source

The world can contain:

- civilized settlements and factions;
- tribal groups;
- savage creatures;
- undead;
- solitary or independent beings;
- magical creatures;
- unexplored wild regions.

These categories align with the concrete enemy roster and should remain data-driven rather than hardcoded as presentation assumptions.

## Protagonist presentation boundary

The source contains detailed visual and thematic characterization for the protagonist. The translated layer preserves only the gameplay-independent requirement that the protagonist has a distinct identity and supports multiple presentation states. Physical appearance, wardrobe, and theme-specific behavioral preferences are presentation data and are not required by the implementation model.

## Narrative invariant

The durable story structure is:

`exploration -> conflict -> alternative or conventional resolution -> relationship/resource/progression outcome -> community growth -> access to new opportunities`

This invariant is compatible with the existing regional-act structure and base progression model.
