# AHTW — Art Direction & Asset Review Pack

**Audience:** art director, character artist, concept artist, CG illustrator, UI artist, asset producer.  
**Purpose:** allow art review without reading combat code or the full GDD.

## 1. Product and visual premise

*Alya Heals The World* is a single-player, turn-based narrative RPG with community/base management, exploration, recruitment, relationships, and adult themes. Its world is humanoid high fantasy first. Species, culture, gender presentation, personality, social role, and gameplay function are separate design axes.

The desired overall presentation is **strongly feminine in visual language**, while preserving distinct silhouettes, body types, species cues, social roles, and personalities. The cast should feel like inhabitants of one coherent fantasy world—not a collection of unrelated prompt experiments.

All characters are adults and must read unambiguously as adults. Avoid child-coded proportions and terms, including “loli,” “shota,” or “petite” as a shorthand for youth. Do not infer age, consent, personality, or role from gender presentation or species.

## 2. Art families to plan

1. **Character identity assets:** full-body reference, portrait/expression set, combat representation where applicable, outfit variants.
2. **Narrative CGs:** story-critical illustrations and optional adult/sensual scenes.
3. **World/environment:** community hubs, regional exploration locations, facilities, landmarks, encounter spaces.
4. **Enemies and bosses:** readable silhouettes, threat hierarchy, variants, telegraphed intent where gameplay needs it.
5. **Items/equipment:** icons and, only where useful, larger inventory/equipment illustrations.
6. **UI and information design:** party, skills, status effects, resource/facility states, quest choices, assignment and Expedition results.
7. **Production support:** reusable background sets, expressions, pose libraries, effects, localization-safe text layouts, source files and export conventions.

A prompt is not evidence that an asset exists. Every requirement needs a status such as **KEEP / REFINE / REPLACE / MISSING / NOT REQUIRED**, linked to a stable content ID and a real file path once one exists.

## 3. Visual guardrails

- Humanoid fantasy is the baseline; beastfolk are a minority, not the default for every character.
- Species should have clear, reusable visual grammar. A species label alone is not enough: specify anatomy, face, ears/horns/tail/skin/markings, and limits on fur or animal traits.
- Keep silhouettes distinguishable at thumbnail scale. Do not use body shape as the only differentiator.
- Outfit design should communicate role, region, resources, and lived experience. Avoid modern details unless deliberately justified by canon.
- Character art should support personality and function, not just a shared erotic template.
- Adult content is optional. It must not be required for main quest completion, endings, recruitment, or access to core progression.
- Do not frame coercion, institutional abuse, corruption, or a character's vulnerability as automatic consent or as a required erotic reward.
- Keep the Ferida visually unsettling and legible as a corruption/manipulation of existing vulnerabilities; avoid making every social or material injustice “just magic.”
- Preserve visual continuity across full-body references, portraits, CGs, sprites, and outfit variants.

## 5. CG and illustration policy

The existing CG matrix is an initial idea inventory, not a final production schedule or asset registry. Before commissioning/generating at scale, each proposed scene should have:
- stable scene/character IDs;
- narrative purpose and act/quest location;
- adult-only cast confirmation;
- consent and agency framing;
- required vs optional status;
- camera/pose/emotion/setting direction;
- outfit/continuity requirements;
- unlock conditions if relevant;
- variants/crops/resolution and UI placement;
- a real status and owner.

Prioritize story-critical illustrations and gameplay readability before large volumes of optional CGs. The Act I vertical slice calls for at most one optional adult CG; it is not required for completion.

## 6. Environment and UI anchors

The first vertical slice, **“The Water We Share,”** is set in Brackenford and the Ashfen Verge. Important visual anchors:
- Brackenford as a lived-in settlement under water/resource pressure;
- old irrigation channels, patched infrastructure, the Old Weir and Root-Vault Sluice;
- the **Red Thread**, a local name for red filament-like Ferida growth around the irrigation system;
- three contrasting initial facilities: Recovery Room (care/stability), Common Workshop (repair/conversion), Coordination Board (visibility/delegation).

Avoid designing the Red Thread as a complete explanation for the water conflict: scarcity and unfair allocation predate the manifestation.

## 7. What is not yet locked

Do not assume the following are complete without a separate production pass:
- file-by-file asset inventory and asset ownership;
- final art style bible, palette, rendering pipeline, canvas/resolution standards;
- exact portrait/sprite/CG counts per character;
- complete environment and UI asset lists;
- final naming/versioning/export conventions;
- audio and localization visual requirements;
- final CG unlock/state rules;
- all outfit variant designs and production priorities.

## 8. Review checklist

- Does each character read as an adult, and is the silhouette distinctive?
- Are species cues clear without overwhelming the humanoid fantasy baseline?
- Does the outfit fit role, region, and lived experience?
- Can a character be recognized across portrait/full-body/CG variants?
- Is the asset required for gameplay/story, optional, or merely speculative?
- Does a proposed adult scene preserve agency and remain optional?
- Does the visual treatment preserve the story's material/political causes rather than attributing everything to supernatural corruption?
- Is every approved asset traceable to a stable ID and actual file?

## 10. Sensual / relationship CG planning baseline

CGs are proposed story/relationship assets, not confirmed production files. The current matrix uses three emotional beats per listed relationship character: **(1) tease/character-specific invitation, (2) consensual intimacy/affection, (3) aftermath showing emotional continuity or changed behavior.** It does not imply every route or unlock is implemented.

### Ally-specific scene motifs

- ALLY-002: training challenge; willingly sets aside shield and accepts affection; quiet rest after armor is removed.
- ALLY-003: teasing competition; mutually chosen kiss after an honest argument; jokes to conceal vulnerability afterward.
- ALLY-004: distracted magical study; consensual closeness during a shared experiment; tries to take notes the next morning but is smiling.

### CG production rules
- Level/beat 1 should establish character and emotional invitation; beat 2 should show reciprocal agency and clear consent; beat 3 should show a character-specific consequence, trust, humor, comfort, or changed behavior rather than simply more revealing clothing.
- Keep scenes non-graphic in the production brief; exact rating and implementation require a separate content review.
- Use character-specific materials, settings and props; sensuality should not homogenize the cast into one goth/fishnet template.
- Generate sequentially: establish identity/tonality first, pair visual language second, emotional aftermath third.
- Camera: cut-ins use half/three-quarter framing; tease scenes use full-body/three-quarter composition; intimacy uses medium framing with faces/hands readable; aftermath uses medium/wide framing emphasizing emotional continuity.
- All participants must be adults; consent and agency must be legible. Rescue, rehabilitation, corruption, power imbalance or vulnerability is never automatic consent.
- Route availability, partner identity, unlock flags, exact asset IDs, animation-vs-still choice, variants and final dialogue are not locked by the concept matrix.

## 11. Asset inventory and production workflow

Asset families: character references, portraits/expressions, outfits, combat representations, CGs, environments, facilities, enemies/bosses, items/equipment icons, UI states, effects, backgrounds, audio, localization-safe layouts and export/source files.

For every planned asset, track: stable ID; associated character/quest/facility/enemy/item; purpose; required vs optional; variant; source prompt/reference; status; owner; output path; resolution/aspect ratio; acceptance notes; dependencies; and localization/rating constraints.

Use status labels **MISSING / IN PROGRESS / REVIEW / APPROVED / REJECTED / NOT REQUIRED**. Keep a separate prompt status from actual image status. Prioritize assets needed for the vertical slice's readability and quest progression before broad optional CG production. The slice's adult CG is optional and must not gate completion.
