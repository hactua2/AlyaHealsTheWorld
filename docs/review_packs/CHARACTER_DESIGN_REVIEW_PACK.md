# AHTW — Character Design Review Pack

**Audience:** character designer, narrative designer, casting/roster reviewer, relationship writer, art lead.  
**Purpose:** summarize identity, function, relationship, and production rules for the cast.

## 1. Character design principles

- Every named character needs a distinct narrative function, temperament, visual identity, and relationship to the world.
- Species, culture, gender presentation, anatomy, personality, social position, and gameplay function are independent axes.
- All characters are adults and must read unambiguously as adults.
- Allies and NPCs are different role models. NPCs do not join combat while operating as NPCs; a recurring identity may have a separate Enemy/Boss representation if the story requires it.
- Do not create a new NPC to duplicate an ally's unique gameplay or emotional identity.
- Recruitment conditions should be discoverable and understandable once relevant, but need not be exposed before discovery.
- H-content relevance is tiered and elastic; a named character does not automatically require a dedicated CG.

## 4. NPC relevance and progression

- H0 Background: no individual H-content.
- H1 Template: no individual H-content.
- H2 Named Functional: light relationship/H-content only if appropriate.
- H3 Named Recurring: can support full individual H-content.
- H4 Major NPC: full individual H-content and possible dedicated CGs.

These are production/relevance tiers, not moral rankings. A character can become more or less relevant as the story evolves.

NPCs may execute community functions and may modify Expeditions when justified, but do not perform player-driven sidequests independently. Only selected named NPCs may die; character exits/departures should have explicit conditions and consequences.

## 5. Core narrative relationships

- Alya's arc is about learning to distribute responsibility rather than becoming the only person who can save everyone.
- The Organization is internally diverse. The Pragmatic Mentor and Institutional Representative should disagree in method and risk tolerance without reducing either to a fool or villain.
- Community leadership is collective and contested; residents have agency and historical knowledge.
- The Ferida exploits vulnerabilities but does not erase responsibility or explain every injustice.
- Recruitment and rehabilitation must change relationships and social roles in a grounded way, not simply turn enemies into interchangeable party members.

## 6. Review questions

- Can each ally be described without using another ally as the main comparison?
- Does each ally's community role matter outside combat?
- Are any NPCs duplicating a recruitable ally's unique function?
- Does a character's arc follow from their values, limitations and relationships?
- Is the reason for recruitment or departure concrete and legible?
- Are species and presentation represented consistently across prose and art?
- Does optional adult content preserve the character's agency and broader identity?
- Are stable IDs preserved, especially retired NPC-020?

NPCs do not participate in combat while functioning as NPCs. A recurring narrative identity can have a separate enemy/boss representation. Exceptional NPC-to-ally transitions need a concrete narrative/system reason. NPCs can deliver community services and unique Expedition modifiers, but cannot perform Sidequests independently of Alya. NPC progression is lightweight function/relationship state, not deep ally combat progression. Named NPCs can relate to Alya and one another; departures require explicit conditions; only selected named NPCs can die. Recruitment conditions become clear once relevant, but may not be exposed from the start.

NPCs use independent functional tags rather than exclusive categories. Mandatory tags: Quest Giver, Quest Target, Vendor, Narrative Antagonist, Recruitment Candidate, World-event Participant. Desirable: Information/Rumors, Faction Representative, Mentor/Trainer. Optional: Authority, Specialist, Social/Relationship. Population targets: roughly 15–25 named recurring, 8–15 reusable templates, elastic background population; there are 12 generic NPC templates in the current full-game baseline. Dense hubs should feel populated and exploration should be punctuated by NPC encounters.

Faction planning target is 4–6; the current baseline is six. Faction state combines reputation and narrative states. Important outcomes may absorb a faction/community into Alya's community. Some antagonists are conversational and do not require a combat encounter.

## 9. H-content tiers and global identity rules

- H0 Background: no individual H-content.
- H1 Template: no individual H-content, except template-level implications if later justified.
- H2 Named Functional: light/relationship-driven content where appropriate.
- H3 Named Recurring: may support full individual content.
- H4 Major NPC: full individual content and possible dedicated CGs.

Tiers are about narrative relevance and production scope, not morality; a character can be promoted/demoted without architectural change. Dedicated CGs are primarily for H4 and the most important NPCs.

Every character must be clearly adult. No child-coded proportions, adolescent styling, school-uniform coding, or use of “petite” as a design category. Adult body diversity is encouraged. The world is humanoid high fantasy first; beastfolk are a minority and should remain humanoid rather than animal-dominant. Species, culture, faction, personality, presentation and gameplay function are separate axes. Desert Elf is a visual/cultural subspecies without automatic mechanical differentiation. Visual taxonomy should not generate unnecessary stats/aptitude branches. Each named-character reference should record species/subspecies, presentation, adult age bracket, height class, body type, distinctive traits and generation constraints.

Adult/intimate content is optional and does not gate main progression, recruitment or endings. Rescue, rehabilitation, corruption, institutional abuse, vulnerability or power imbalance is never automatic consent. Keep agency and relationship continuity legible.
