# AHTW — Character Design Review Pack

**Audience:** character designer, narrative designer, casting/roster reviewer, relationship writer, art lead.  
**Purpose:** summarize identity, function, relationship, and production rules for the cast.

## 1. Character design principles

- Every named character needs a distinct narrative function, temperament, visual identity, and relationship to the world.
- Species, culture, gender presentation, anatomy, personality, social position, and gameplay function are independent axes.
- All characters are adults and must read unambiguously as adults.
- Allies and NPCs are different role models. NPCs do not join combat while operating as NPCs; a recurring identity may have a separate Enemy/Boss representation if the story requires it.
- Do not create a new NPC to duplicate an ally's unique gameplay or emotional identity.
- Recruitment conditions should be discoverable and understandable once relevant, but need not be exposed before discovery.
- H-content relevance is tiered and elastic; a named character does not automatically require a dedicated CG.

## 4. NPC relevance and progression

- H0 Background: no individual H-content.
- H1 Template: no individual H-content.
- H2 Named Functional: light relationship/H-content only if appropriate.
- H3 Named Recurring: can support full individual H-content.
- H4 Major NPC: full individual H-content and possible dedicated CGs.

These are production/relevance tiers, not moral rankings. A character can become more or less relevant as the story evolves.

NPCs may execute community functions and may modify Expeditions when justified, but do not perform player-driven sidequests independently. Only selected named NPCs may die; character exits/departures should have explicit conditions and consequences.

## 5. Core narrative relationships

- Alya's arc is about learning to distribute responsibility rather than becoming the only person who can save everyone.
- The Organization is internally diverse. The Pragmatic Mentor and Institutional Representative should disagree in method and risk tolerance without reducing either to a fool or villain.
- Community leadership is collective and contested; residents have agency and historical knowledge.
- The Ferida exploits vulnerabilities but does not erase responsibility or explain every injustice.
- Recruitment and rehabilitation must change relationships and social roles in a grounded way, not simply turn enemies into interchangeable party members.

## 6. Review questions

- Can each ally be described without using another ally as the main comparison?
- Does each ally's community role matter outside combat?
- Are any NPCs duplicating a recruitable ally's unique function?
- Does a character's arc follow from their values, limitations and relationships?
- Is the reason for recruitment or departure concrete and legible?
- Are species and presentation represented consistently across prose and art?
- Does optional adult content preserve the character's agency and broader identity?
- Are stable IDs preserved, especially retired NPC-020?
