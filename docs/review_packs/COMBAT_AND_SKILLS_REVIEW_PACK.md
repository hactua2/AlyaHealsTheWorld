# AHTW — Combat & Skills Review Pack

**Audience:** combat designer, skill designer, enemy/encounter designer, balance reviewer.  
**Purpose:** summarize the approved combat model and skill-catalog decisions.

## 1. Combat identity

Combat is turn-based, deterministic in its resolution order, and driven by skill-defined targeting rather than grid position or independent range. Builds emerge from skills, stats, equipment, passives, modifications and choices; the game does not require rigid classes.

The player should be able to read enemy intent where designed and use defensive/reactive actions to respond. Encounters can require more than reducing enemy health: outcomes may include defeat, disable, capture, yield, retreat, or objective completion.

## 2. Core meters and tactical resources

- **Vitality:** primary defeat meter.
- **Strain:** secondary pressure meter; supports thresholds, alternate outcomes, reactive mechanics and Overflow.
- **Command:** generated by directive/manipulation actions; spent to alter behavior, reduce resistance or prepare Break.
- **Resolve:** generated through deliberate exposure/risk; enables responses and alternate finish paths.
- **Pressure:** generated by exploiting weaknesses, vulnerable states or unstable targets; improves damage/debuffs.
- **Fortitude:** generated by receiving damage, resisting pressure or surviving critical situations; spent for defense, retaliation and survival.

Canonical vocabulary is neutral and mechanical. Thematic labels are presentation/localization and must not redefine mechanics.

## 3. Keywords and action model

Core keywords include **Exploit, Consume, Chain, Threshold, Overflow**. Skills may share implementation templates without losing distinct identities when their scaling, requirements, costs, synergies or tactical hooks differ.

- Validate requirements and resource costs before committing an action.
- Insufficient resources make an action unavailable.
- An atomic action completes its declared effect/reaction pipeline before terminal state is committed.
- Reactions/chains/threshold checks follow deterministic order.
- Initiative is randomized once at encounter start, with Agility modifying the initial result; turn order then remains fixed.
- Surrendered and Incapacitated are distinct states, but both prevent acting and count as out of active combat.
- Combat ends when every member of one side is Surrendered and/or Incapacitated.
- AI preserves intent/action if a target becomes invalid, retargeting where possible and otherwise using an explicit fallback.

## 4. Effects and passives

Effects have definitions and runtime instances, with source, target, start/duration, stacks, strength, tags and removal rules. Strongest equivalent effect wins by default unless explicit data overrides stacking. Equipment-derived effects disappear immediately when their granting equipment is removed; cursed equipment follows its explicit curse contract.

Passives use declared categories such as CorePassive, BuildPassive and ConditionalPassive. Derived stats recalculate after relevant mutations. State mutations commit before immutable domain events publish.

## 5. Skill catalog and approved migration

- Current canonical skill catalog: **67 records**.
- Primary catalog: `data/skills/skills.csv`; complex definitions: `data/skills/skill_definitions.json`.
- Stable skill IDs must be preserved unless a reviewed migration plan explicitly changes them.
- H-gauge semantics migrate to Strain where applicable; Health Damage maps to damage to Vitality, but each effect must be checked against its intended meaning.
- P-growth is a Capability/Transformation that enables dependent skills.
- Tiers represent both unlock/progression and expected power.
- Unlocks combine Skill Points, prerequisites and progression gates.
- Preserve the legacy fantasy/intention, but merge or remove only genuine mechanical redundancy. Enslave was consolidated as an alias of Absolute Control in the normalized catalog.

Do not invent new skills to meet a quota or copy complex mechanics into vertical-slice data when a canonical skill definition already exists.

## 6. Encounter design baseline

Full-game content lock: 35 enemy archetypes, 9 major bosses, 8 special encounters. This is a content inventory, not a claim that every encounter is tuned or runtime-tested.

Act I introduces:
- **Frayed Husk:** slow direct pressure; teaches target selection.
- **Threadling Swarm:** faster Strain/pressure application.
- **Hollow Warden:** defensive elite with visible intent; rewards control/preparation.
- **Root-Knot Anchor:** objective entity; pressure continues until the sluice objective is completed.

The climax is objective-based: stabilize the diversion, sever the active filament, and secure the gate. Simply eliminating enemies is insufficient.

## 7. Balance and QA status

Catalog normalization and structural validation are documented. Do not claim actual runtime balance or Unity playtest completion from a static catalog check. Numerical values, encounter difficulty, skill synergies, AI behavior and edge-case interactions require implementation-level tests and playtesting.

Balance review should include:
- every skill's action economy, cost, targeting, requirements and effect semantics;
- Strain thresholds and alternate outcomes;
- resource generation/spending loops;
- control and defensive response counterplay;
- multi-target and objective encounter behavior;
- progression/unlock feasibility;
- AI fallback and terminal-state ordering;
- interactions with equipment, passives and temporary effects.

## 8. Review checklist

- Does each skill have a distinct tactical reason to exist?
- Are costs, targeting, requirements, effects and scaling explicit?
- Does a skill's thematic label match its actual effect without redefining it?
- Are alternate combat outcomes handled intentionally?
- Can objective encounters be completed through the intended actions?
- Are resource loops exploitable or degenerate?
- Are effects and reactions ordered deterministically?
- Are numeric claims marked provisional until runtime testing?
