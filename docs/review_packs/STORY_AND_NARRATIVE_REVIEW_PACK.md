# AHTW — Story & Narrative Review Pack

**Audience:** story editor, narrative director, worldbuilder, narrative systems designer.  
**Purpose:** make the current story canon and five-act shape reviewable without opening implementation documents.

## 1. Core premise

Alya arrives as an outsider in a community already under pressure. Her journey begins as a promise/responsibility she is partly trying to outrun. She learns to help people without becoming the single point of failure for every community she encounters.

The central thematic movement is:
**avoid responsibility → accept responsibility → centralize too much → distribute responsibility → create conditions that survive without Alya at the center.**

The world is not a simple battle between good communities and evil institutions. Communities have scarcity, unequal access, old resentments and internal disagreement before supernatural pressure arrives. The Organization is often competent and necessary, but its protocols and incentives can make it accept losses that are locally devastating.

## 2. Canonical conflict: communities, Organization, Ferida

### The Ferida
The Ferida amplifies vulnerabilities, fear, exploitation and failed correction. Its local manifestations may corrupt, distort, or exploit existing systems and relationships. It should not:
- explain every injustice;
- erase the agency or responsibility of people affected;
- function as generic mind control;
- guarantee that defeating a monster permanently resolves the underlying problem.

### The Organization
The Organization provides resources, protection, infrastructure, specialists, training and regional connections. In exchange it expects supervision, protocols, information sharing and regional responsibility. It is internally diverse: pragmatic allies, institutional representatives, hardliners and researchers can disagree for credible reasons.

It is not the central evil faction. Its choices should be technically defensible in some dimensions while still exposing blind spots and unacceptable distributions of risk.

### Community evolution
Informal survival → hybrid leadership → delegated responsibilities → formal representation → regional cooperation network.

## 3. Five-act spine

1. **Act I — Foundation / “The Water We Share.”** Alya reaches Brackenford in the Ashfen Verge. A water-allocation dispute around Mara Venn predates the Red Thread, a local Ferida manifestation in an old irrigation system. The Organization proposes containment that protects downstream settlements but abandons lower residents and the seed reserve. Alya builds a third option from local knowledge, supplies, care and coordinated action. The lower-terrace seed reserve and part of the next planting cycle are lost regardless. Brackenford survives; Alya accepts accountable leadership; the first autonomous Expedition begins.
2. **Act II — Construction / “A Place for the Uncounted.”** Brackenford grows as a refuge and formal community. Assignments, representation and political structures develop. The Organization treats the settlement as a pilot/training centre and study subject. A relocation order targets people the community shelters; Brackenford refuses, creating a rupture with the Organization.
3. **Act III — Consequences / “The Cost of Being Free.”** Autonomy creates resource shortages, trade leverage, regional disagreement and legitimacy problems. Mycelian memory and interregional cooperation become important. The answer is a voluntary mutual-aid compact across regions, not isolation.
4. **Act IV — Escalation / “What the Wound Learns.”** The Ferida exploits signals, governance, trust and triage at systemic scale. Evacuation and network fracture challenge the coalition. Alya's old instinct to centralize decisions returns. The Shattered Archive reframes what the world thought it understood.
5. **Act V — Revelation and Resolution / “A Future Without a Center.”** Alya's past and the Ferida Manipulator come into focus. The coalition's commitments matter mechanically and narratively. The Confluence is a major final encounter. Resolution depends on distributed responsibility, with five ending families rather than one universal “perfect” outcome.

The full-game lock contains 47 main quest stages, superseding the earlier estimate of 45, plus 25 authored sidequests across seven regions.

## 4. Act I vertical slice — story beats

The vertical slice is titled **“The Water We Share.”** Core locations are Brackenford, Ashfen Verge, Silt Path, Old Weir and Root-Vault Sluice. The Red Thread is a local name, not a complete explanation of the Ferida.

Eleven quest stages cover:
1. arrival and the empty cistern;
2. water allocation and community history;
3. first Red Thread investigation/encounter;
4. the cost of a quick fix;
5. Alya's decision to stay;
6. the Organization's containment proposal;
7. preparation of a third option;
8. objective-based sluice climax;
9. consequences and the seed-reserve loss;
10. accountable leadership and a facility choice;
11. the first autonomous Expedition.

Mandatory loss is intentional. No dialogue choice reverses it and no character death is added merely to make the outcome feel serious.

## 5. Tone and dramatic rules

- Grounded, compassionate, specific. Do not make every scene a speech.
- Let characters disagree about evidence, priorities and risk; avoid making one side stupid solely to make Alya look correct.
- The Ferida influences interpretation through fear, exhaustion, fixation and distorted inference; it does not remove agency.
- Alya's past is hinted through brief reactions in Act I; do not reveal the whole truth early.
- Choice labels state the player's intent honestly even if consequences are uncertain.
- Do not grant instant forgiveness after harm or loss. Accountability, trust and recovery need time.
- A “third option” should be built from evidence, people, materials and trade-offs—not a miraculous no-cost solution.
- Adult themes are part of the world, but intimacy is optional and not a required reward for progress, recruitment, rescue or endings.

## 6. Narrative state and consequence philosophy

Choices may alter relationships, quest flags, encounter preparation, recruitment/integration, resources, facility access, and faction standing. A choice must have a legible intention and an actual downstream consequence where promised. The exact stable choice IDs and state mutations live in the quest data; dialogue prose must not become state identity.

Main quests and sidequests are direct player activities. Expeditions are autonomous community-delegated activities, not sidequests performed off-screen by the protagonist.

## 7. Open narrative gates

The five-act outline and base inventory are authored, but do not imply final production completion. Remaining narrative work includes:
- complete final dialogue for all 47 main stages and 25 sidequests;
- exact choice effects, reward IDs/values and state mutations for every stage;
- character-by-character arc and relationship continuity review across all acts;
- faction voice and regional cultural specificity;
- consistency review for all endings and their preconditions;
- asset/CG scene mapping and localization review.

## 8. Review checklist

- Is the injustice present before the Ferida manifestation?
- Does the Ferida intensify rather than erase agency?
- Are the Organization's arguments credible, including when Alya opposes them?
- Does each act change the scale and nature of Alya's responsibility?
- Are costs concrete, persistent and not instantly forgiven?
- Does community growth distribute responsibility rather than only increase Alya's authority?
- Are choices honest about intent and meaningful downstream?
- Is optional adult content separated from coercion and mandatory progression?
