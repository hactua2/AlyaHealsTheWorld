# AHTW — Skills Audit & Reconciliation

> Status: **STRUCTURE CLOSED; CATALOG RECONCILIATION IN PROGRESS**. This document records approved design decisions and first-pass recommendations. It does not claim the concrete catalog, balance, implementation, or QA are complete.

## 1. Purpose and audit method

The skill system should create recognizable gameplay loops rather than a collection of isolated actions. Builds should generate and spend resources, exploit tagged states, react to enemy intent, manage risk, and pursue different encounter outcomes.

Audit order:
1. Resolve canonical mechanical vocabulary.
2. Define tier semantics and unlock rules.
3. Reconcile legacy entries and the 14 previously approved additions.
4. Give every retained skill a stable ID and structured mechanics.
5. Validate coverage, redundancy, progression, balance, and encounter outcomes.
6. Commit the finalized CSV and companion data only after the reconciliation decisions are reviewed.

## 2. Approved decisions

### 2.1 Canonical vocabulary

The implementation identifiers in `implementation_spec/README.md` and `implementation_spec/COMBAT.md` are the sole mechanical source of truth. Presentation names may be thematic or localized, but they must not introduce parallel mechanical resources.

| Canonical identifier | Mechanical meaning | Legacy terminology policy |
|---|---|---|
| Vitality | Primary defeat/health meter | Use for the primary defeat meter; presentation text may say health. |
| Strain | Secondary pressure meter; thresholds and alternate outcomes | Legacy H-gauge terminology must be mapped only after its exact intended meaning is confirmed. |
| Command | Tactical control resource | Replace legacy Control when it means a spendable tactical resource. |
| Resolve | Resource generated through deliberate exposure/risk for reactive builds | Legacy Surrender is not a synonym. Surrender is an outcome/state where defined. |
| Pressure | Resource generated by exploiting weaknesses or unstable states | Replace Cruelty only where it means exploitable-state advantage. |
| Fortitude | Resource generated by enduring damage/resisting pressure | Replace Endurance when it means the defensive/reactive resource. |
| Break | Prepared state that reduces resistance and enables special outcomes | Do not use as a generic synonym for stun, disable, or capture. |
| Overflow | Excess Strain beyond its normal threshold | Avoid treating it as a second meter unless separately specified. |
| Aspect | Tagged target domain used by skills and modifiers | Body-part targeting may be represented through Aspect tags where appropriate; confirm mapping in content data. |

Other canonical shared keywords: **Exploit, Consume, Chain, Threshold, Overflow**. These are mechanics/keywords, not resources.

### 2.2 Tier semantics and unlock contract

Tiers express **both progression expectation and expected power**, not raw power alone.

| Tier | Expected progression | Power/design expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Early access; low or modest prerequisites | Reliable foundation, focused effect, limited setup and manageable opportunity cost. |
| Tier 2 | Requires Skill Points plus meaningful prerequisites; may require a milestone or content gate | Stronger efficiency, specialization, synergy, or flexibility; meaningful condition/cost should justify it. |
| Tier 3 | Significant investment, prerequisite chain, or later progression gate | Build-defining impact, major resource conversion, exceptional utility, or high risk/reward. |

Unlock evaluation combines Skill Points, declarative prerequisites, and progression gates. Narrative gates should be used when early access would destabilize pacing or when the skill is tied to a discovered capability. Avoid arbitrary level-only rules and hidden mandatory requirements. Use the existing declarative requirement contract in `implementation_spec/CONTENT_CONTRACTS.md` and milestone model in `implementation_spec/PROGRESSION_AND_GATING.md`.

A higher tier is not guaranteed to dominate every lower-tier skill in every context. Tier review considers impact, reliability, action economy, resource cost, setup, flexibility, and encounter relevance.

### 2.3 Catalog source of truth

- `data/skills/skills.csv` remains the **primary concrete skill catalog**.
- The CSV must gain stable IDs and normalized, machine-readable mechanical fields.
- `implementation_spec/SKILL_CATALOG.md` defines the schema and shared primitives.
- Companion files are allowed for complex effect sequences, formulas, requirement graphs, presentation/cut-in references, or reusable definitions that would make CSV cells opaque.
- The CSV must not depend on natural-language descriptions to define runtime behavior.
- Stable IDs are immutable identity; presentation names can change.
- No bulk rewrite of the legacy CSV is approved until the first-pass reconciliation is reviewed. This avoids silently locking in incorrect assumptions.

### 2.4 Legacy content policy

Preserve the fantasy and player-facing intention where useful, but **reformulate, merge, or remove mechanically redundant entries**. Retaining every old entry is not a requirement. New generic skills should not be added unless a demonstrated gap remains after reconciliation and testing.

## 5. First-pass redundancy and differentiation audit

These are review hypotheses, not automatic deletions.

| Cluster | Entries | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Control and target restriction | Flirt, Hypnotize, Bind, Sensory Deprivation, Bully, Command, Break Will/BreakResistance, Enslave/AbsoluteControl | Preserve only when tactical roles differ: resource generation, intent manipulation, accuracy/defense debuff, incapacitation, Break preparation, or encounter outcome. Avoid several skills that merely “control” with different prose. |
| Damage bait and reactive defense | Get Spanked, Instill Protectiveness, Provoke, Endure, Countertease/Counter, Moans, Turn the Tables, Unbroken | Give each a unique timing/decision: redirect intent, deliberately invite attack, mitigate, retaliate after a declared trigger, reflect a portion, convert recent vulnerability, or prevent a defined defeat condition. |
| Repeated damage / vulnerability | Bend, AS, Aphrodisiac, Prolonged Pleasure, Pink Mist, Exploit Weakness | Distinguish single-target stacking/repetition, target behavior changes, Strain threshold interaction, area pressure, and tagged weakness exploitation. Merge if the only difference is damage-over-time text. |
| Finishers and outcomes | Swallow, Bkakke, Enslave, Absolute Control | Define different eligibility and outcome semantics (single-target threshold, all-target threshold, control-resource conversion, capture/disable/yield). A finisher should use the shared encounter outcome system rather than independently ending combat. |
| Overfill/Overflow | S Symbol, Ecstasy Aftershocks, Overflow Engine | Separate defensive recovery, reactive area effect, and explicit resource conversion. Ensure Strain/Overflow definitions are used consistently and avoid duplicating the same trigger. |
| Transformation prerequisites | P-growth, Autof, F-fk, Penetrate, A-penetration | Treat P-growth as a capability/transformation if it changes the available action set, not just a generic buff. Define a shared requirement/tag and distinguish each dependent skill's tactical purpose. |
| Physical/Magical | Many entries | Element/type must change targeting, defenses, costs, tags, or interactions—not only a label or a second formula. |
| Passive effects | Sadism, Masochism, Otherworldly Charm, Piercing Stares, Cuteness, Naughty Thoughts, C-drinker, Divine Health, Extra Tips, etc. | Classify as CorePassive, BuildPassive, or ConditionalPassive. Ensure no passive duplicates a permanent stat/effect already owned by equipment or status data. |
| Repeated names/tags | Bully and Bind appear in multiple conceptual families | A skill has one canonical record and can carry multiple tags. Do not create duplicate records just to place one skill in multiple archetypes. |

## 6. Tier and progression review rules

For each skill, record:
- tier;
- Skill Point cost;
- declarative prerequisites;
- optional milestone/content gate;
- whether it is active, passive, capability/transformation, or another explicitly supported skill kind;
- resource cost and generation;
- expected action economy;
- conditions, target query, effects, cooldown/chain/threshold/overflow rules;
- encounter outcome interactions;
- relevant Aspect/state tags;
- presentation name and description.

Tier is reviewed against expected impact, reliability, flexibility, setup, cost, and availability. The system should not assume that Tier 3 means “highest damage”; an advanced skill can be defensive, utility-focused, or high-risk.
