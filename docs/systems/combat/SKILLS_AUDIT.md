# AHTW — Skills Audit & Reconciliation

> Status: **STRUCTURE CLOSED; CATALOG RECONCILIATION IN PROGRESS**. This document records approved design decisions and reconciliation recommendations. It does not claim the concrete catalog, balance, implementation, or QA are complete.

## 1. Purpose and audit method

The skill system should create recognizable gameplay loops rather than a collection of isolated actions. Builds should generate and spend resources, exploit tagged states, react to enemy intent, manage risk, and pursue different encounter outcomes.

Audit order:
1. Canonicalize mechanical vocabulary.
2. Define tier semantics and unlock rules.
3. Reconcile legacy entries and the 14 previously approved additions by functional cluster.
4. Give every retained skill a stable ID and structured mechanics.
5. Validate coverage, redundancy, progression, balance, and encounter outcomes.
6. Commit the finalized CSV and companion data only after proposed dispositions are reviewed.

## 2. Approved decisions

### 2.1 Canonical vocabulary and meter migration

The implementation identifiers in `implementation_spec/README.md` and `implementation_spec/COMBAT.md` are the sole mechanical source of truth. Presentation names may be thematic or localized, but must not introduce parallel mechanical resources.

| Canonical identifier | Mechanical meaning | Legacy terminology policy |
|---|---|---|
| Vitality | Primary defeat/health meter | Use for the primary defeat meter; presentation text may say health. |
| Strain | Secondary pressure meter; thresholds and alternate outcomes | Map legacy H-gauge to Strain; validate the meaning of every effect before converting. |
| Command | Tactical control resource | Replace legacy Control when it means a spendable tactical resource. |
| Resolve | Resource generated through deliberate exposure/risk for reactive builds | Legacy Surrender is not a synonym. Surrender is an outcome/state where defined. |
| Pressure | Resource generated by exploiting weaknesses or unstable states | Replace Cruelty only where it means exploitable-state advantage. |
| Fortitude | Resource generated by enduring damage/resisting pressure | Replace Endurance when it means the defensive/reactive resource. |
| Break | Prepared state that reduces resistance and enables special outcomes | Do not use as a generic synonym for stun, disable, or capture. |
| Overflow | Excess Strain beyond its normal threshold | Use the shared Overflow event/contract; do not treat it as a second meter unless separately specified. |
| Aspect | Tagged target domain used by skills and modifiers | Body-part targeting may be represented through Aspect tags where appropriate; validate mappings in content data. |

**Approved migration rule:** legacy `H-gauge` maps to canonical `Strain`; legacy `Health Damage` maps to damage to `Vitality`. This is a terminology rule, not permission to blindly copy old effects. Validate target, direction, timing, scaling, resistance/mitigation, trigger order, duration, and encounter-outcome behavior for each row. Where one legacy description combines several mechanics, split it into ordered structured effects.

Other canonical shared keywords: **Exploit, Consume, Chain, Threshold, Overflow**. These are mechanics/keywords, not resources.

### 2.2 Tier semantics and unlock contract

Tiers express **both progression expectation and expected power**, not raw power alone.

| Tier | Expected progression | Power/design expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Early access; low or modest prerequisites | Reliable foundation, focused effect, limited setup and manageable opportunity cost. |
| Tier 2 | Requires Skill Points plus meaningful prerequisites; may require a milestone or content gate | Stronger efficiency, specialization, synergy, or flexibility; meaningful condition/cost should justify it. |
| Tier 3 | Significant investment, prerequisite chain, or later progression gate | Build-defining impact, major resource conversion, exceptional utility, or high risk/reward. |

Unlock evaluation combines Skill Points, declarative prerequisites, and progression gates. Use the existing contracts in `implementation_spec/CONTENT_CONTRACTS.md` and `implementation_spec/PROGRESSION_AND_GATING.md`. Avoid arbitrary level-only rules and hidden mandatory requirements. A higher tier is not guaranteed to dominate every lower-tier skill in every context.

### 2.3 Catalog source of truth

- `data/skills/skills.csv` remains the **primary concrete skill catalog**.
- The CSV must gain stable IDs and normalized, machine-readable mechanical fields.
- `implementation_spec/SKILL_CATALOG.md` defines the schema and shared primitives.
- Companion files may define complex effect sequences, formulas, requirement graphs, presentation references, or reusable definitions.
- Natural-language descriptions are not runtime source-of-truth.
- Stable IDs are immutable identity; presentation names can change.
- No bulk rewrite of the legacy CSV is approved until the reconciliation decisions are reviewed.

### 2.4 Legacy content and consolidation policy

Preserve the fantasy and player-facing intention where useful, but reformulate, merge, or remove mechanically redundant entries. **Consolidation is by functional cluster, and a merge is justified only when player decision and mechanical result are genuinely redundant.** Similar theme alone is not sufficient reason to remove a skill. New generic skills should not be added unless a demonstrated gap remains after reconciliation and testing.

### 2.5 Capability/Transformation policy

`P-growth` is modeled as a **Capability/Transformation** candidate that grants a stable capability tag and enables dependent skills. It is not an ordinary temporary buff by default. The final record must define target scope, acquisition/unlock, persistence, removal policy, save/load behavior, and all dependent skill references. The current dependency candidates are Autof, F-fk, Penetrate, and A-penetration. The legacy row's Ally targeting conflicts with its described role and must be explicitly resolved before migration.

## 6. Requirements for final content lock

Every retained skill needs:
- stable ID and display name;
- kind (Active, Passive, Capability/Transformation, or another registered kind);
- tier and Skill Point cost;
- declarative prerequisites and optional milestone gate;
- target query, actor/target scope, and valid target conditions;
- canonical resource costs/generation;
- structured effects and conditions;
- duration, tick timing, stack/refresh policy, cooldown, Chain/Threshold/Overflow references where applicable;
- encounter-outcome interactions;
- Aspect/state tags;
- presentation reference;
- testable acceptance condition.

Tier is reviewed against impact, reliability, flexibility, setup, cost, and availability—not just damage.
